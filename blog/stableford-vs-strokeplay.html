Stableford or strokeplay? Picking a format for your four-ball

Stableford forgives the blow-up hole and levels the field with handicaps. Here's why it suits a friendly golf league, and how to agree a format on the first tee.

← From the course
Formats8 October 2026· 4 min read

Every group has one: the mate who three-putts the first and spends the next seventeen holes in a mood. The format you pick can make or break the round for everyone, and a good league format keeps every player in the game until the last putt drops.

Strokeplay is the purest test, but a single bad hole can wreck a card. Stableford forgives it. Pick up after a blow-up, take the zero and move on to the next tee with your points still alive.

Why Stableford suits a league

  • Bad holes don't sink you. The most you can lose on a hole is the points you'd have scored.
  • Handicaps level the field. Strokes are given on the hardest holes, so a 24 can take on a 4.
  • Rounds move faster. No one grinds out a triple bogey when the points have gone.
Bad holes don't sink you. That's the whole point.

Whichever format you choose, agree it on the first tee and write it down. Arguments over the rules cost more rounds than any slice ever did.
