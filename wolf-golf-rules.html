Wolf golf rules

How to play the Wolf golf game, and exactly how Honors scores it: picking partners, lone Wolf, points and the Wolf Cap.

How Wolf works Rules and points Wolf Cap Other game modes

How Wolf works

Wolf is a four-player game where one player each hole is the Wolf: they watch the others drive, then pick a partner or go it alone. Honors works out every point from the scores and the Wolf’s calls.

Scottie Scheffler* has talked publicly about playing Wolf with his friends at home, describing it as a game with plenty of volatility that makes it fun.

* Honors is not affiliated with or endorsed by Scottie Scheffler.

Set it up

Choose Wolf as the scoring mode when you start a round. Wolf needs exactly four players. Guests count, so it works with just one phone.

Betting is switched off in Wolf. Points are the whole game.

Round details

The round details show Wolf as the game being played, so everyone in the group knows what is on.

Settings and default points

The host can change each point value from 1 to 12 in Wolf settings. The defaults are:

  • 2 v 2: each winner takes 1 point from each loser, so the winning pair get +1 each and the losing pair −1 each.
  • Lone Wolf: 3 points per opponent. A lone Wolf who wins gets +9 and each opponent −3. If they lose, they pay 3 to each opponent (−9 total) and each opponent gets +3.
  • Blind Lone Wolf: 6 points per opponent (±18 for the Wolf).

Every hole is zero-sum: what the winners take is exactly what the losers pay.

Who is the Wolf?

The Wolf rotates, and a badge on the hole map shows who it is this hole. Holes 1 to 16 follow the seating order: the host first, then the others in the order they were added.

On 18-hole courses, hole 17 goes to whoever is in last place on points and hole 18 to whoever is third. If players are level, it goes to whoever was Wolf most recently.

Picking a partner

The Wolf tees off first, then the others follow. After seeing the drives, the Wolf can pick a partner or pass on each player in turn. Passing on all three makes them a lone Wolf.

Who wins the hole

Each hole is a contest between two sides: the Wolf’s side and the other side. If the Wolf picks a partner, it is the Wolf and their partner against the other two players. If the Wolf goes lone, it is the Wolf against the other three.

  1. Take each player’s score for the hole (net by default, or gross if the host has switched it).
  2. On each side, only the lowest score counts. The higher score on that side is ignored, so a partner’s bad hole doesn’t drag the side down.
  3. Compare the two. The side with the lower score wins the hole.
  4. If the two sides’ best scores are equal, the hole is a push and nobody gains or loses points.

Worked example. Sam is Wolf on hole 3 and picks Jo after seeing her drive. Sam scores 4 and Jo scores 6, so their side’s score is 4, the lower of the two. The other pair score 5 and 7, so their side’s score is 5. 4 beats 5, so Sam and Jo win the hole: they each get +1 and the other two get −1.

Blind Lone Wolf

For bigger stakes, the Wolf can declare lone before anyone has teed off. This is only possible before any call has been made on that hole, and the host can turn it off.

Reading the points

The scorecard has a Wolf column and the points after each hole. The points are always worked out from the scores and the Wolf’s calls, so correcting a score or a call updates every hole.

Net or gross, and rollover

  • Net or gross (a setting, net by default). On net, handicap strokes are worked out from the lowest handicap in the group: each player gets the difference between their handicap and the lowest one, on the hardest holes first.
  • Rollover (a setting, off by default). When on, a tied hole adds 1 extra point to the next hole that has a winner. Any rolled points still undecided at the end of the round are dropped.

Winning Wolf: the Wolf Cap

A Wolf round has a winner only when every hole has been decided (a win or a push) and the best total is above zero. The player with the most points wins.

The winner’s screen shows the winner, the points table and “Wolf Cap unlocked”. The cap is a hat for their character that they can put on, and take off, in their profile. Friends see it on their character.

A guest who wins is named on the winner’s screen but can’t unlock the cap, because it belongs to an account.

Ties

If players tie on the best total, they all win, and each gets the Wolf Cap.
